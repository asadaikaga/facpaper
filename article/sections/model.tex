\section{Our model problem and numerical experiments}

Two class of toeplitz matrices are used for analysing. They can be expressed in the following structures.
The first class of toeplitz matrices has diagonal entries of value $2$, and subdiagonals of value $1$ and $\gamma$ at position $1$ and $-2$, respectively. They are mathematically written as the form $\mathcal{A}$ in \eqref{toemat}.
The second class of toeplitz matrices has the same diagonal entries as the first class of matrices. However, the subdiagonals are of value $-1$ and $\delta$ at position $1$ and $-2$, respectively. They are mathematically written as the form $\mathcal{B}$ in \eqref{toemat}.

\begin{equation}\label{toemat}
	\mathcal{A}: \mqty{
	2 & 1& & & & \\
	0& 2& 1& & & \\
	\gamma&0 & 2& 1& & \\
	& \ddots&\ddots & \ddots& \ddots& \\
	& & \gamma&0 & 2& 1\\
	& & & \gamma& 0& 2
},
\quad
\mathcal{B}:
\mqty{
	2 & -1& & & & \\
	0& 2& -1& & & \\
	\delta&0 & 2& -1& & \\
	& \ddots&\ddots & \ddots& \ddots& \\
	& & \delta&0 & 2& -1\\
	& & & \delta& 0& 2
}
.
\end{equation}

The behavior of Krylov subspace methods on problem $\mathcal{A}$ and problem $\mathcal{B}$ can be divided into three categories.

The model problem is chosen for the following reasons. First, the behaviour of Krylov subspace methods is very complicated on different types of problems.
